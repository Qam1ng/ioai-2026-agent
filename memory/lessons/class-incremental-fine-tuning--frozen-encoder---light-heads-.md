For class-incremental classification from a pre-trained checkpoint under a tight compute budget (e.g. ~10 min in-kernel training), a fully frozen encoder with cached embeddings + light heads beat partial fine-tuning of the top transformer layers (CV 0.911 vs 0.891, LB 0.807 vs 0.799 on AST audio, 920 clips, 16->29 classes). Why it wins: (1) freezing IS the anti-forgetting mechanism — old-class accuracy was higher than with fine-tuning, and old-class oversampling / lower encoder LR / label smoothing all HURT the fine-tuned model; (2) cached embeddings make each classifier experiment take seconds, enabling ~10x more ablations per hour. Recipe that worked: sliding-window embeddings (window = model max_length, stride = half) with per-clip softmax averaging; warm-start the expanded head from the checkpoint's old-class rows (old rows LR x0.1, new rows = class-mean init); blend a warm-started linear head with a small MLP on concat(pooled, mean) features (~0.3/0.7); sample weights = clip-equal x class^-0.5 x old/new group-balanced. Also: ultra-rare classes (n<=3-7) can be a hard 0%-accuracy floor that BOTH diverse models share (identical confusions), so ensembling won't fix them — check per-class confusion before spending budget there. Only trust local CV deltas larger than ~1 fold-std; ensemble gains within noise (e.g. +0.001-0.002 on 4 folds) rarely transfer to LB.
